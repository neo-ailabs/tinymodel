You are benchmarking an on-device language model inference runtime designed to scale from ultra-constrained microcontrollers and single-board computers up to desktop workstations. Consider three concrete hardware tiers and analyze the architectural trade-offs involved in supporting all three from a single Rust codebase.

Tier 1 (Micro): Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, featuring a quad-core ARM Cortex-A53 in-order processor running at 1.0 GHz with ARMv8.0 NEON SIMD (no dotprod, no native FP16 arithmetic), 512 MiB of shared LPDDR2 SDRAM (~350 MiB usable after the Linux kernel and minimal userspace), and roughly 1.6 to 2.2 GB/s of sustained memory bandwidth. Only sub-500M parameter models fit without swapping to the SD card. Because embedding tables in modern tokenizers (150k to 262k tokens) can consume over half the parameter budget of a 270M model, memory-mapped zero-copy weight access and offline vocabulary pruning are mandatory.

Tier 2 (Edge): Raspberry Pi 5 and mid-range smartphones, featuring out-of-order Cortex-A76 or Cortex-A78 cores with ARMv8.2 dotprod (and I8MM on newer mobile SoCs), 4 to 8 GiB of LPDDR4X/LPDDR5 memory, and 11.5 to 40 GB/s of sustained DRAM bandwidth. Models between 0.8B and 4B parameters run interactively at 4-bit quantization. Here, single-token decode already reaches 70% to 90% of the theoretical bandwidth roof in mature C++ engines, whereas prompt prefill often achieves less than half of peak integer throughput, leaving substantial room to reduce time-to-first-token via cache-blocked micro-kernels and online activation quantization.

Tier 3 (Host): Desktop workstations and Apple Silicon laptops (such as Apple M4 with unified memory, NEON/dotprod/I8MM/SME2, and Metal GPU compute, or x86_64 systems with AVX-512 and discrete Vulkan/CUDA GPUs), offering 16 GiB+ RAM and 100 GB/s to 1 TB/s bandwidth. Models up to 8B–9B parameters run comfortably, and long-context key-value caches rival model weights in memory footprint.

Task: Summarize the five highest-leverage engineering optimizations across prefill, decode, state management for hybrid linear-attention layers, memory planning, and verification testing.
